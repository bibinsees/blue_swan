A Game About
Tokenization

Can you control what a language model says — using as few words as possible?

Tokenization is one of the most fundamental yet often overlooked components of large language models. The way text is split into tokens directly affects model efficiency, memory usage, and performance. This becomes especially important for applications such as coding assistants, on-device LLMs, or systems working with less common programming or natural languages.

ERIC is a project supported by JetBrains Research. We are improving tokenization algorithms and adapting tokenizers for new domains and languages.

To make this concept tangible, we created a small experimental game. The game challenges you to prompt a language model to produce a specific phrase consisting of predefined tokens. However, there is a twist: you are not allowed to use any of the words contained in that phrase. Instead, you must describe the desired output indirectly while using as little text as possible.

This seemingly simple task quickly turns into a non-trivial puzzle. You will discover that controlling the token-level behavior of a language model is much harder than expected, and that the choice of words in the prompt strongly interacts with the model's internal tokenization.

By turning tokenizer mechanics into a game, we offer an intuitive way to explore how language models process text and why tokenization design matters for real-world AI systems.
